# Atlantic Vagrancy Guild v2 — expansion review

**GBIF occurrence snapshot: 2026-11-18.** This is a screening recommendation only; the active species registry and all weather/ship-assistance scores remain unchanged.

## Selection method

Reviewed 40 candidate taxa from the supplied list using the production GBIF taxon matcher and production occurrence filters for GB, IE, NL, BE and DK. Queries required a strict GBIF species match, human observations, coordinates, no geospatial issue, post-1940 dates (1941 onward), and excluded records explicitly marked captive, cultivated or managed. All 40 names matched an accepted species exactly with GBIF confidence 99/100.

A canonical event is one **species + valid date + latitude rounded to 0.01° + longitude rounded to 0.01°**. GBIF records are deduplicated by occurrence key before event counts are calculated. Annual coverage is the number of distinct calendar years with qualifying records; it is not an estimate of population or accepted rarity-committee individuals.

Recommendations combine event count, spread across target countries and years, known vagrant plausibility, and escape/taxonomic cautions. The 18 additions below are ranked by event count, then country count and annual coverage; the guild mix is intentionally expanded beyond the already passerine-heavy baseline. High GBIF counts are not treated as proof that each record is wild or accepted. Missing captive/establishment metadata can leave escapes in the data, and one bird reported on multiple dates forms multiple date-keyed events.

## Limitations and sources

- GBIF occurrence records are not rarity-committee decisions. Country totals include observer reports, and date-location events can include the same bird on separate dates.
- Establishment/captive filtering only removes records whose metadata explicitly identify captive, cultivated or managed origin. Review flags therefore remain important, particularly for Ring-necked Duck and Bufflehead.
- GBIF name-match confidence (99, exact accepted species) describes the scientific-name match to the GBIF backbone; it does not validate field identification or wild provenance.
- Green-winged Teal is matched by GBIF to accepted *Anas carolinensis* (key 2498125); taxonomy can treat it as a species or an American subspecies of Eurasian Teal, so keep that taxonomic concept explicit if the registry is later changed.
- [GBIF Species API](https://techdocs.gbif.org/en/openapi/v1/species) and [Occurrence API](https://techdocs.gbif.org/en/openapi/v1/occurrence) define the match and occurrence services used.
- [BTO BirdFacts](https://www.bto.org/learn/about-birds/birdfacts) provides Britain/Ireland status evidence; examples include [Song Sparrow](https://www.bto.org/learn/about-birds/birdfacts/song-sparrow), [White-crowned Sparrow](https://www.bto.org/learn/about-birds/birdfacts/white-crowned-sparrow), [American Golden-Plover](https://www.bto.org/learn/about-birds/birdfacts/american-golden-plover), [Buff-breasted Sandpiper](https://www.bto.org/learn/about-birds/birdfacts/buff-breasted-sandpiper), [White-rumped Sandpiper](https://www.bto.org/learn/about-birds/birdfacts/white-rumped-sandpiper), [Baird's Sandpiper](https://www.bto.org/learn/about-birds/birdfacts/bairds-sandpiper), [Pectoral Sandpiper](https://www.bto.org/learn/about-birds/birdfacts/pectoral-sandpiper), [Lesser Yellowlegs](https://www.bto.org/learn/about-birds/birdfacts/lesser-yellowlegs), [American Wigeon](https://www.bto.org/learn/about-birds/birdfacts/american-wigeon), [Surf Scoter](https://www.bto.org/learn/about-birds/birdfacts/surf-scoter), [Ring-billed Gull](https://www.bto.org/learn/about-birds/birdfacts/ring-billed-gull), [Franklin's Gull](https://www.bto.org/learn/about-birds/birdfacts/franklins-gull), and [Laughing Gull](https://www.bto.org/learn/about-birds/birdfacts/laughing-gull).
- [BTO BirdFacts: Ring-necked Duck](https://www.bto.org/learn/about-birds/birdfacts/ring-necked-duck) describes frequent escapes; [Bufflehead](https://www.bto.org/learn/about-birds/birdfacts/bufflehead) notes difficulty separating wild and escaped birds; [Lesser Scaup](https://www.bto.org/learn/about-birds/birdfacts/lesser-scaup) notes identification difficulty; [Black Scoter](https://www.bto.org/learn/about-birds/birdfacts/black-scoter) notes its recent taxonomic recognition.
- [BOU British List](https://bou.org.uk/british-list/) explains the categories used to distinguish established wild records from uncertain or captive-origin taxa; the Yellow-headed Blackbird status account is [here](https://www.rushcliffewildlife.co.uk/bb/species_accounts/yellow_headed_blackbird.html).
- [BirdGuides: Grackles in the Western Palearctic](https://www.birdguides.com/articles/britain-ireland/grackles-in-the-western-palearctic/) documents the Netherlands Common Grackle record and discusses vagrancy context.
- The Dutch [CDNA 2024 annual report](https://www.dutchavifauna.nl/static/jaarverslagen/CDNA-verslag_2024.pdf) provides national rarity-committee context for accepted Dutch records.
